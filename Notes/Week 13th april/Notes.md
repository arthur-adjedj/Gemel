Spent the week making toy implementations of STLC, one intrinsically typed, the other redoing what was done in the Coq à La Carte case study, the other taking `lean-stlc` and trying to extend it. The latter will hopefully be a great proof-of-concept, since the library uses many Lean niceties like instances, tactic automations like `simp` that produce non-trivial proof term to translate, and has a general enough notion of binders (à la AutoSubst), allowing me to not bother with not fighting too much when dealing with fundamental lemmas about renamings and substitutions. 
One big issue I have been having is wrt mvar unification. In many cases in proof-terms, some previously modmapped def appears unfolded in the term, often as a match, often leading to a new mvar being produced for said match rather than simply recognise the match as the previous def. The good news is that proof terms have so little holes that unification easily figures out what the solution for such matches-turned-mvar is, the bad news is it fails to unify it since said mvars are synthetic opaque, which are never meant to be solved by `isDefEq`. I tried for a long time to instead initialize such vars as naturals, but the internals of lean aren't meant to handle that well. In particular, delayed mvar assignments don't behave the same at all on non-synthetic-opaque mvars, leading to lots of headaches ! I spent at least 1 day trying different things, until I figured out `withAssignableSyntheticOpaque` existed and did exactly what I wanted... 
Well, it supposedly does exactly what I want, but in practice unification still gets stuck on "obvious" mvars, presumably because said mvars are produced in the wrong scope somehow. I am not done investigating that, but it is in progress !
I also fixed many, many, many bugs along the way, and made the `mod_def` syntax nicer when working in a new workspace `Foo`, allowing a user to write `mod_def extends bar` rather than `mod_def Foo.bar extends bar`.

I've also been thinking about the fact that, when extendion some inductions, it might make sense to want to generalize such an induction "after the fact", i.e when working on an mvar deeper than said induction. According to Yannick, this is an interesting problem worth focusing on more seriously in the future. For now, I simply have two ideas: 
1. Like with matches, abstract away the entire induction behind an mvar to allow more control. I think this would clash with the usual tactic system, which I don't want, and would be very weird to handle when working with many nested inductions/cases, especially if some of those inductions that need generalizing aren't done on a term that got partial mapped
2. Make some sort of zipper on expressions, allowing one to "walk back" on a term from a given mvar to try and generalize past inductions as much as possible. I think this would be the most general and interesting way to go at it, and I wanna work with Zippers so..

Inductive/function functors are still a pipe dream. I think inductive functors will be trivial in their shape, and for now we'll only have one kind of such functor anyway, namely something taking an inductive with specific parameters/indices and adding new ctors. The plan for the future would be add new such functors, namely:
- Adding/removing some fields from existing ctors
- Adding/removing parameters/indices (this would need to be composed with adding new fields to ctors to make anything non-trivial work e.g list to vectors)
